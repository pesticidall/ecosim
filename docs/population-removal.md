# Population removal

Pause play and select a habitat. In the right-hand **Interventions** tab, choose
a species, choose 1–10 animals, and inspect the date, cost, and population change. The
apply button commits the action immediately on the current unresolved date.
Changing selections or inspecting a preview spends nothing.

Every action costs 3 IC and consumes the one global removal charge, shared by
all species and habitats. The charge recovers after two unique completed month
boundaries. For example, a January action has one recovery boundary at January
completion and becomes available after February completion. Acknowledging review,
reopening a report, retrying a failed date, or requesting the same month's
recovery again cannot accelerate it. Enough IC is still required when the charge
returns. These timings and costs remain provisional balance targets.

The player chooses a whole-animal quantity, starting at 1 and capped at 10 per
action. Removal also cannot exceed the selected local population. Invalid input
is rejected rather than clamped or rounded. The 3 IC cost and single charge apply
to the action regardless of quantity. Animal definitions need no intervention
preset fields; any living local animal species can be removed. This replaces the
earlier species-defined preset design.

The engine validates the target, date, population totals, condition cohorts, IC,
and charge before committing anything. It updates local and regional counts,
reconciles survivor injuries through deterministic proportional allocation, and
records the species, quantity, IC, charge use, date, and event ID in the shared
intervention history. No random draws, carcasses, or predation/starvation records
are generated by removal. Partial removals retain aggregate rounding carry;
removing the entire local population clears its pending fractions.
For species using nutrition, removal also takes proportional stored food and
preserves mean deficit-days among survivors; extinction clears nutritional state.
Stored food is not refunded or converted into an extra carcass.

Monthly reports list removal separately from deaths. Their opening population
includes animals removed before the first day's ecology, so the population ledger
still reconciles. Subsequent ecological consequences come from the normal daily
systems. Population introduction is a separate action described in
`docs/population-introduction.md`.
